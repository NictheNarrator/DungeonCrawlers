# Watercolor Pixel — the approved style

This folder is the visual bible. The images here are the authoritative reference
for every asset in Dungeon Crawler World; `ART-DIRECTION.md` is the working
summary of them. Where the summary and an image disagree, the image wins.

## The rules these set

- **16px base**, previewed at 4×. Nearest-neighbour scaling only: no blur, no
  antialiasing, no smooth gradients, no airbrushing.
- **One limited palette** — the strips on pages 04 and 05 give the fourteen;
  the master sheet gives sixteen. Large pixel clusters, soft watercolour colour
  relationships, colour used in blocks rather than blended.
- **Simplified, readable shapes** with strong silhouettes, top-down or slightly
  angled perspective.
- **Effects** ship at 16×16 and 32×32. **Item icons** at 24×24.
- **Portraits** are framed, on pale simple grounds, with a name plate, a role
  line and a quote.
- **Bright, warm and rich**, even in dangerous rooms. The world is colourful and
  inviting; the things that happen in it are not. That contrast is the point, so
  never answer a dark scene with a dull palette.

## Casting, as locked

- **Mara** — survivor / leader. "We keep moving. That's the rule."
- **Tobin** — veteran / protector. The big bearded man from the portrait page.
- **Vex** — scavenger / rogue, and an adult woman. Dark blue hair, red scarf.
  "Rules are suggestions. Loot isn't."
- **Eli** — tech / tactician. Glasses, worn jacket. "Information keeps us alive."
- **June** — scout / support. Cream-blonde hair up, green hood.
  "There's still good people down here."
- **Skrit** — the ratman has no portrait page yet; design him from the ratman
  sprites on the master sheet so he belongs to the same set.

Mara, June and Vex are three adult women who must read as three different people:
sharp and capable, warm and soft-featured, cool and striking. Attractiveness comes
from face, expression, confidence, hair and silhouette — never from the clothing.

## Relationship to the older guide

`art/floor1-style-guide.png` was the earlier, darker tile-and-prop guide. Its
Floor 1 *layout* thinking still stands; its palette and surface treatment are
superseded by the Watercolor Pixel pages here.
